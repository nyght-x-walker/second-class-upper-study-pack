PHYSIOLOGICAL MECHANISMS

Biology Behind Fungal Economic Value



Polarised Hyphal Growth: Apical tip extension penetrates solid wood, soil, and grain
matrices.

Absorptive Heterotrophy: Extracellular amylases, cellulases, proteases, and ligninases
digest polymers externally.

Secondary Metabolism: Produces high-value organic acids, antibiotics, statins, and
mycotoxins.

Dual Economic Edge: Identical enzymatic digestion recycles forest litter yet spoils stored
crops.

PLANT–FUNGAL MUTUALISM

Mycorrhizae: Underground Nutrient Trading



                            Host Plant (Photosynthates / Sugars) ⇄ Fungus (Phosphorus, Zinc & Water)



                         Arbuscular Mycorrhizae (Glomeromycota): Form intracellular arbuscules in ~80% of crops
                         including maize and cassava.

                         Ectomycorrhizae (Basidiomycota/Ascomycota): Form a hyphal mantle and Hartig net around
                         timber tree roots.

                         Agronomic Caveat: Commercial inoculants require field validation; high soil phosphorus
                         suppresses colonization.

FUNGAL HORTICULTURE

Edible Mushrooms: Nutrition and Value Chains



Major Cultivated Taxa: Pleurotus ostreatus (oyster), Lentinula edodes (shiitake), and
Agaricus bisporus (button).

Nutritional Profile: Rich in β-glucan dietary fiber, B-vitamins, potassium, and digestible
protein (15-30% dry weight).

Value-Chain Tiers: Pure-culture spawn labs → substrate growers → fresh markets, dried
slices, and mushroom powders.

Foraging Safety: Never identify wild mushrooms by color alone; Amanita amatoxins cause
fatal hepatotoxicity.

FOOD BIOTECHNOLOGY

Yeasts in Baking and Fermented Foods


                            C6H12O6 (Glucose) → 2 C2H5OH (Ethanol) + 2 CO2 (Leavening Gas)


                         Pure Yeast Processes: Saccharomyces cerevisiae drives bread leavening, brewing, and palm
                         wine fermentation.

                         Mixed Consortia in Ghana: Maize doughs (kenkey, banku) rely on lactic acid bacteria co-
                         fermenting with yeasts (Candida, Saccharomyces).

                         Economic Value: Extends shelf stability without refrigeration and enhances B-vitamin
                         bioavailability.

SECONDARY METABOLITE THERAPEUTICS

Life-Saving Pharmaceuticals Derived from Fungi



Penicillins (Penicillium rubens): β-Lactam antibiotics inhibiting bacterial peptidoglycan synthesis.


Cephalosporins (Acremonium chrysogenum): Broad-spectrum β-lactams resistant to many β-lactamases.


Lovastatin (Aspergillus terreus): HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor lowering serum LDL cholesterol.


Cyclosporine A (Tolypocladium inflatum): Calcineurin inhibitor preventing organ transplant rejection.

AGRICULTURAL LOSSES

High-Impact Fungal Crop Diseases Worldwide



                         Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae): Destroys enough rice annually to feed over 60 million
                         people.

                         Wheat Stem Rust (Puccinia graminis): Virulent Ug99 lineage threatens East African and
                         global wheat harvests.

                         Coffee Leaf Rust (Hemileia vastatrix): Defoliates Arabica coffee, slashing smallholder
                         export revenue.

                         Postharvest Moulds (Botrytis, Penicillium): Cause 10–30% transit and storage losses in
                         fruits and grains.

GHANA CASE STUDY · CRITICAL TAXONOMIC DISTINCTION

Cocoa Black Pod: Oomycetes vs. True Fungi



Causal Agents: Phytophthora megakarya (aggressive West African species) and P.
palmivora.

Stramenopila (Not Fungi): Oomycetes possess cellulose cell walls, diploid hyphae, and
biflagellate zoospores.

Epidemiology: Rain splash transfers soil zoospores onto low pods, causing rapid brown-
black necrosis.

Economic Toll: Unmanaged wet-season outbreaks destroy 30-90% of pods on affected
Ghanaian farms.

GHANA CASE STUDY · 03 · MYCOTOXIN ECONOMICS

Aflatoxin Contamination in Maize and Groundnut



                                              Toxigenic Producers: Aspergillus flavus and A. parasiticus colonize drought-stressed or
                                              damp grain.

                                              Public Health Hazard: Aflatoxin B1 is a Group 1 liver carcinogen causing child stunting and
                                              acute toxicosis.

                                              Trade & Feed Losses: Export rejection at EU borders and Aflatoxin M1 carryover into dairy
                                              milk.

                                              Safety Rule: Invisible toxins persist after cooking; chemical ELISA or lateral-flow testing is
                                              mandatory.
