            Course Content Descriptions
• Definition, history, scope and branches of mycology; general attributes of
  fungi; differences between fungi, plants, bacteria and animals; ecological
  distribution of fungi.

• Beneficial and harmful fungi; roles in agriculture, medicine, biotechnology,
  food production, decomposition, industrial fermentation, mycotoxin
  production and human diseases.

• Thallus organization; unicellular and filamentous fungi; hyphae, septate and
  aseptate hyphae, mycelium, pseudohyphae, dolipore septum and clamp
  connections.

• Plectenchyma, prosenchyma, pseudoparenchyma, sclerotia, rhizomorphs,
  haustoria, hypopodia, appressoria, ectomycorrhizae and plasmodia.
           Course Content Descriptions
• Heterotrophic nutrition; saprotrophic, parasitic and mutualistic fungi;
  extracellular digestion; nutrient absorption; environmental factors
  influencing fungal growth.

• Fragmentation, budding, fission, sporulation; sporangiospores, conidia,
  chlamydospores, arthroconidia and blastoconidia; asexual reproductive
  structures and diagrams.

• Plasmogamy, karyogamy and meiosis; homothallism and heterothallism;
  sexual spores including zygospores, ascospores and basidiospores;
  sexual life cycles.

• Principles of classification; taxonomic hierarchy; binomial nomenclature;
  traditional versus molecular classification; major fungal phyla and
  identification characteristics.
              Course Content Descriptions
• Traditional Myxomycota (slime moulds), Chytridiomycota and related groups;
  characteristics, vegetative structures, reproduction, life cycles, ecological and
  economic importance.

• Traditional Zygomycota and modern classification; Rhizopus, Mucor and related
  genera; reproductive structures, life cycles, food spoilage and mucormycosis.

• General characteristics; asci and ascospores; ascocarps; sexual and asexual
  reproduction; life cycles of selected taxa; Aspergillus, Penicillium, Saccharomyces
  and selected tropical pathogens.

• General characteristics; basidia and basidiospores; basidiocarps, dikaryotic
  mycelium, clamp connections; mushroom life cycles; rusts, smuts and selected
  tropical plant pathogens.

• Dermatophytes, Candida, Cryptococcus, Histoplasma, Sporothrix and major
  tropical crop pathogens; classification, disease significance.

                    Attributes of Fungi
• Reserve food: glycogen and lipids (not starch)

• Body: unicellular yeast or filamentous (hyphae
  forming mycelium)

• Reproduce by spores, asexual and sexual

• Habitat: soil, dead matter, water, plants, animals;
  saprophytes, parasites, symbionts

• Kingdom Fungi is closer to animals than to plants
  (shared chitin, glycogen, true nuclei -
  chromosomes).
